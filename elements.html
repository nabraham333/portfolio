Assignment 3: World Happiness Report — Good vs. Bad Visualization

Data Source: World Population Review — Happiest Countries in the World

Good Data Visualization: Top 10 Happiest Countries

Why this visualization works:

  • Intuitive Sorting & Type: A horizontal bar chart allows country names to be read naturally without tilting your head. Sorting scores in descending order makes visual comparisons immediate.
  • High Data-to-Ink Ratio: Unnecessary decorative elements, heavy 3D shading, and visual noise are removed, allowing key data points to stand out clearly.
  • Accessible Scale: The horizontal axis starts at an appropriate baseline and uses consistent spacing, preserving scale integrity.

Bad Data Visualization: Overstuffed Happiness Distribution

Why this visualization fails:

  • Wrong Chart Selection: Pie charts rely on judging visual angles and area, which humans do poorly compared to length. Representing relative score differences across many categories fails completely.
  • Excessive Cognitive Load: Cramming dozens of categories into a single color wheel creates an unreadable rainbow smudge with overlapping labels.
  • Misleading Proportions: Similar score values (e.g., 7.74 vs 7.52) appear visually indistinguishable when converted to tiny wedge slices.
